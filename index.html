One local stack

Four systems you already sell. One deployment.

Most biometric kiosks stop at “verified.” Sentry Access carries the decision through the door, the camera stream, the audit trail, and the phone in your client’s pocket.

1:N face verification

Local face matching on a Windows kiosk with liveness prompts, quality gating, and a configurable distance threshold. Templates never leave the site.

  • Encrypted 128-d templates
  • Encryption key in the OS keystore
  • Offline enrollment and matching

Door control with rules

Pulse an Axis network door controller or a generic HTTP relay only after identity and policy both pass. Schedules, holidays, and role rules are evaluated locally.

  • Axis A1210/A1601 VAPIX
  • Fail-closed on controller error
  • Manual unlock for commissioning

Camera intelligence

Pull existing RTSP or NVR streams into the local face engine. Log only matched enrolled identities, with a per-camera cooldown to prevent duplicate observations.

  • RTSP, HTTP MJPEG, NVR channels
  • Bundled FFmpeg — no separate install
  • Frames never stored or uploaded

Audit that travels

Every grant, denial, camera observation, and door command lands in a searchable local audit trail, then syncs to the LAN hub and the iPhone companion.

  • CSV export for client reporting
  • Signed identity bundles
  • Kiosk inventory and health

Local vault, local keys

Biometric templates are AES-GCM encrypted at rest. Controller and camera credentials live in the operating-system keystore, not in the database or browser storage.

  • DPAPI-backed Windows keystore
  • Credentials excluded from sync
  • No image retention

Optional LAN hub

A durable on-prem hub handles identity distribution, audit consolidation, and mobile pairing. If the hub is down, the kiosk keeps verifying and queues events.

  • PostgreSQL-backed local hub
  • Delta sync with retry queue
  • Backups and stable hub ID

Privacy is the sales feature

“Where does the face data go?” has a short answer.

Nowhere. Templates are encrypted on the kiosk, matching happens locally, and only the resulting decision is synced. Camera gateway observations are limited to already-enrolled identities — unknown passersby are not identified or logged.

Data minimization

No image retention. No raw video upload. Audit events carry the identity, decision, camera or door label, confidence, and time — not biometric payloads.

Credential isolation

RTSP URLs and controller passwords are encrypted through the OS keystore and excluded from IndexedDB, CSV exports, audit payloads, and mobile data.

Recoverable by design

The kiosk keeps operating if the hub is offline, queues audit events, and reconciles later. Backups cover the database, keys, and enrollment secret.

What this release does not claim

  • Not a certified access-control or life-safety product. Use fail-safe hardware, independent REX, and fire-alarm release.
  • No REX/door-position supervision, held/forced-door alarm, anti-passback, or two-person rule yet.
  • No automatic ONVIF discovery and no Ring support. Cameras and NVRs use manual RTSP/HTTP URLs.
  • Liveness is a basic challenge-response gate, not certified presentation-attack detection.
  • Manual unlock is audited but not yet tied to an operator identity or role.
  • The Windows installer is unsigned until a code-signing certificate is configured.
  • Biometric consent, signage, retention, and DPIA obligations remain with the deploying organization.

Procurement questions

The answers your client will ask for.

Does the system need internet access?

No. Verification, door policy, camera processing, audit storage, and the LAN hub all run on site. Internet is only needed if you choose to expose the hub remotely, and that is not part of this release.

Which cameras and NVRs are supported?

Any source that exposes an RTSP, RTSPS, or HTTP MJPEG URL, including NVR channel streams. The bundled FFmpeg handles the decode. Automatic ONVIF discovery and Ring are not included.

Which door hardware does it control?

AXIS A1210/A1601 network door controllers through VAPIX, or a generic HTTP relay endpoint. Wire the relay through a fail-safe strike with independent request-to-exit and fire-alarm release. Hardware is not included.

What happens if the network or controller fails?

The door command fails closed. The kiosk keeps running and queues audit events; it does not fall back to an open state. Site design should still include mechanical egress and local fire release.

Is the iPhone app required?

No. It is a read-only companion for audit review and kiosk status. Verification and door control happen on the Windows kiosk. The iOS app still needs a signed build for distribution.

Can we white-label it?

Not in this release. Founding integrators get a demo deployment, joint commissioning, and a pilot report you can put in front of your client. Full white-label packaging is evaluated with multi-site agreements.

How is biometric compliance handled?

Sentry Access minimizes data and keeps matching local, but the deploying organization remains responsible for consent, notice, retention, access requests, and any BIPA, GDPR, CCPA, or state-specific obligations. A DPIA and counsel review are required before production use.

What is included in the paid pilot?

A two-week single-site deployment: remote commissioning, one kiosk, up to two doors and four camera streams, policy testing, a fail-closed verification, and a written pilot report. Travel and hardware are separate.

How do refunds and licensing work?

The pilot fee is credited toward a single-site license. Software licenses are per site and delivered electronically. See the terms for the current policy; contact us before purchase if procurement needs a specific clause.
